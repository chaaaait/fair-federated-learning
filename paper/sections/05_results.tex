\section{Results}\label{sec:results}
All numbers are means $\pm$ standard deviations over three seeds; accuracies and gains are in percentage points
(pts). All 147 runs finished, and every one of the 111 private runs spent its target budget to within 0.001.

\subsection{How Differential Privacy Redistributes Accuracy (M1)}
DP does not cost every client the same (Fig.~\ref{fig:m1acc}). Moving DP-FedAvg from no privacy to $\eps_0=2$ lowers
mean client accuracy by 13.1 pts (40.3\% to 27.2\%) but worst-10\% accuracy by 18.3 pts (30.4\% to 12.1\%); at
$\eps=0.3$ the worst-10\% clients are left at 2.5\% while the mean is still 15.8\%. The spread grows accordingly
(Fig.~\ref{fig:m1fair}): Jain's index falls from 0.976 without DP to 0.865 at $\eps_0$ and 0.703 at $\eps=0.3$, and
the standard deviation across clients rises from 6.3 to 10.5 pts. The worst-10\% figure is also the least stable
across seeds ($\pm$6.2 pts at $\eps_0$), because which clients end up at the bottom depends on the noise draw.

Fine-tuning the DP global model locally removes most of this disparity. Its mean accuracy falls only from 59.4\% to
51.8\% between no DP and $\eps_0$, and its worst-10\% accuracy from 39.8\% to 36.2\%. Its gain over DP-FedAvg
\emph{grows} as the budget shrinks, from 19.1 pts without DP to 24.6 pts at $\eps_0$ and 31.0 pts at $\eps=0.3$. We
therefore find no budget in the sweep below which fine-tuning stops paying off; the earlier synthetic observation
that it fell behind the DP global model at $\eps=0.3$ is not reproduced on CIFAR-10.

\subsection{Mechanisms at a Common Budget (M2)}
At $\eps_0=2$ every personalised mechanism beats DP-FedAvg by a wide margin (Fig.~\ref{fig:m2bars},
Table~\ref{tab:main}). Ranked by mean gain: local-only training $+29.0\pm1.1$, FedPer $+26.8\pm1.5$, fine-tuning
$+24.6\pm0.8$, Per-FedAvg $+22.7\pm1.0$ and IFCA $+15.6\pm2.1$ pts. On the worst-10\% clients FedPer and local-only
tie at 38.1\%, followed by fine-tuning (36.2\%), Per-FedAvg (32.8\%) and IFCA (24.0\%), against 12.1\% for
DP-FedAvg. All five satisfy the evenness constraint of (\ref{eq:problem}): their Jain indices (0.940--0.960) exceed
$J_0=0.865$. FedPer is the most repeatable (SD 0.4 pts in mean and 0.8 pts in worst-10\% accuracy).

Gains are not universal (Fig.~\ref{fig:gain}). FedPer improved every client in every seed. Fine-tuning, local-only
training and Per-FedAvg each left 1.7\% of client--seed pairs worse off than DP-FedAvg, and IFCA 11.7\%. IFCA did not
collapse to a single cluster: in all 18 IFCA runs the smallest of the three clusters still held 3 to 6 clients at the
end. Its weaker result comes instead from splitting each cluster's training over fewer clients, and it
pays double the communication (Table~\ref{tab:cost}). The striking result is that local-only training, which shares
nothing, has the highest mean accuracy: under record-level DP with this label skew, federation adds no mean accuracy
over training alone, although FedPer matches local training on the worst clients with lower variance.

\subsection{Accuracy Lost per Unit of Privacy}
Above $\eps=2$ the budget barely matters: between $\eps=4$ and $\eps=2$ every mechanism loses at most 1.1 pts of mean
accuracy per unit of $\eps$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:m2sweep}). Below $\eps=1$ the cost rises steeply and unevenly. Between
$\eps=1$ and $\eps=0.5$, IFCA loses 10.1 pts per unit $\eps$, FedPer 8.5, local-only and DP-FedAvg 7.6, fine-tuning
3.7 and Per-FedAvg only 2.0. Per-FedAvg is almost insensitive to the budget: its mean accuracy stays between 49.1\%
and 50.1\% from $\eps=0.5$ to $\eps=8$, about 7 pts below its non-private 56.8\%. The curves therefore cross. At
$\eps=0.5$ the mean ranking is local-only (49.7\%), Per-FedAvg (49.1\%), fine-tuning (48.6\%) and FedPer (47.4\%), and
on the worst-10\% clients Per-FedAvg (28.9\%) and fine-tuning (28.6\%) overtake local-only (24.6\%) and FedPer
(22.7\%). The mechanisms that adapt a shared model with a few local steps degrade most gracefully at small budgets.

\subsection{Does a Fairness Objective Help under DP? (M3)}
It does not, in our setting (Fig.~\ref{fig:qffl}). Without DP, q-FFL already lowers both mean and worst-10\%
accuracy of the global model: from 40.3\%/30.4\% at $q=0$ to 29.5\%/9.7\% at $q=1$ and 23.6\%/2.7\% at $q=5$. At
$\eps_0$ it fails outright: mean accuracy is 12.5\% ($q=1$) and 12.1\% ($q=5$), the worst-10\% clients reach 0\%, and
85\% of client--seed pairs end below DP-FedAvg. The validation curves of these runs are flat in every seed (no run
changes by more than 0.5 pts between rounds 10 and 40), so the global model barely moves. The privatised loss reports cost little budget---the noise multiplier
rises by about 2\% (2.06 versus 2.02)---so the failure is not a budget effect; Section~\ref{sec:discussion} gives the
explanation. Combined with FedPer, q-FFL gives 47.0\% mean and 25.4\% worst-10\% accuracy, below plain FedPer
(54.1\%/38.1\%), and $q=1$ and $q=5$ differ by less than 0.1 pts.

\subsection{Measured Leakage versus the Theoretical Budget}
The loss-threshold attack finds almost nothing to exploit (Fig.~\ref{fig:mia}). Across all private runs the mean
AUC lies between 0.500 and 0.509, and at $\eps_0$ the mechanisms are within 0.006 of each other, so an equal $\eps$
did give comparable measured leakage. Without DP, local-only training leaks most (AUC 0.525), followed by fine-tuning
(0.514); both fit a model to one client's data. Because the attack is weak even without DP, these values are lower
bounds on leakage and cannot rank the mechanisms' real privacy risk.

\subsection{Group Fairness on Adult}
On Adult the mechanisms differ little in accuracy (Table~\ref{tab:adult}): 82.8--84.2\% without DP and 82.0--83.2\%
at $\eps_0$, except Per-FedAvg. DP narrows DP-FedAvg's demographic-parity gap between the sexes (20.1 to 14.5 pts)
but widens its equal-opportunity gap (18.3 to 20.1 pts). Personalisation by occupation does not close the gaps under
DP: at $\eps_0$ every working mechanism has a larger demographic-parity gap (16.5--18.8 pts) and a larger
equal-opportunity gap (20.5--25.2 pts) than DP-FedAvg. Without DP the picture differs: local training and
fine-tuning have the smallest equal-opportunity gaps (9.8 and 12.2 pts). Per-FedAvg at $\eps_0$ is a failure case:
in all three seeds it predicts a single class on every client, so its accuracy (76.8\%) is close to the majority
rate and both gaps are exactly zero---a trivially ``fair'' but useless model.

\subsection{Communication and Compute Cost}
Communication separates the mechanisms more than compute does (Table~\ref{tab:cost}). DP-FedAvg and Per-FedAvg move
22.6 MB per round (905 MB in total), and FedPer slightly less (22.4 MB) because the head never leaves the client.
Fine-tuning spends the last tenth of its budget locally and so exchanges 814 MB. IFCA sends all three cluster models
to every client and costs 45.2 MB per round, 1,809 MB in total, for the smallest gain. Local-only training sends
nothing. Per-example DP makes a round 1.4--1.8$\times$ slower (for example 5.4 to 9.5 s for FedPer), and peak GPU
memory stays at 631--642 MB for every mechanism. For bandwidth-limited wireless clients, FedPer and fine-tuning give
nearly the best accuracy at the lowest communication among the federated mechanisms.
